# Launching RitesDev

After several years of thinking about it, I finally did it: **RitesDev is open.**

RitesDev is my freelance development and technical training business. It is something I've been thinking about for years, but for a long time it was just an idea. Now I have a website, a domain, services, and an actual business behind it.

And honestly, it feels a little strange to finally say that.

## Where it started

The idea for RitesDev goes back to 2020, when I was working with some friends on building a community. At the time, I wasn't thinking about starting a development business. I was mostly interested in building things and figuring out what we could create together.

In early 2021, while brainstorming ideas during a call with my friend Malakai, he suggested the name **Rites**.

The idea behind the name was "rites" as in a ceremony or ritual. We were thinking about names for social platforms at the time, and eventually started using names like *SocialRites*.

The name stuck with me.

Eventually, that same idea became **RitesDev** for my development work. Years later, I purchased the domain **RitesDev.dev** and started turning the idea into something much more concrete.

## Actually building the business

I started seriously building the RitesDev website in August 2026, with the goal of launching in September.

The website itself was a much bigger project than I initially expected.

This is the third major version of the site that I've built after restarting and redesigning it. I built it with Astro in VS Code, and because this was my own project, I had to make all of the decisions myself.

That was probably one of the hardest parts.

When you're building a website for someone else, there are usually requirements, preferences, or a design direction to work from. With RitesDev, I was the client, designer, developer, and reviewer.

I had to decide what I wanted the site to look like, how I wanted people to navigate it, what services I wanted to offer, how I wanted to explain those services, and what kind of impression I wanted the business to make.

I'm a developer, not an artist, so figuring out the visual side of the website was definitely a learning experience.

I was constantly asking myself:

> Does this look good?

> Is this easy to navigate?

> Does this actually make sense to someone who isn't me?

Those questions led to a lot of redesigning.

Thankfully, testing the site has given me some good results, which has made all of that work feel worth it.

## More than just development

One of the other things I learned while building RitesDev was just how much goes into running a website and a business outside of writing code.

I spent a lot of time learning about hosting, servers, domains, and everything involved in actually getting a website online and keeping it there.

I also had to think about things like pricing, services, communication, and how to present what I can offer to someone who might want to work with me.

There was a lot more to learn than I expected.

But that's part of why I wanted to do this in the first place.

## Why technical training?

RitesDev isn't only about development.

I also offer technical training and mentorship. That part of the business comes naturally to me because I already teach classes in real life.

I've always enjoyed helping someone understand something they didn't understand before, and development is something I've spent a lot of time learning myself.

Being able to combine those two things—building and teaching—felt like a natural part of RitesDev.

## Finally doing it

The biggest thing about launching RitesDev isn't the website.

It's that I finally stopped talking about doing it and actually did it.

I've been dreaming about having something like this for several years. My wife and my family have encouraged me along the way, and having that support made a huge difference.

There is still a lot I want to improve.

There will be more projects, more blog posts, more changes to the website, and undoubtedly more things I'll have to learn along the way.

But for the first time, RitesDev isn't just an idea I've been talking about.

It's real.

And now that it's launched, I get to see where it goes.
