# Building the Navigation First

I started working on the navigation before I had the rest of the RitesDev website figured out.

That might sound backwards. The navigation is only a small part of the page, and it doesn't tell anyone what the business does. But it gave me a framework for thinking about the site as a group of connected pages instead of a set of unrelated designs.

## It makes the structure visible

When I can see the links together, I have to make decisions about what belongs in the site and what I can leave for later. Home, About, and Contact were a reasonable starting point. The blog could fit alongside them without taking over the whole project.

It also gives me a quick way to check whether the site makes sense. If I can't tell where a page belongs in the navigation, I probably need to think more about why that page exists.

## A useful first step

The navigation wasn't the finished design. It was a small piece I could get working while I was still making larger decisions.

That turned out to be a helpful way to start. I didn't need every answer before I could build something. I just needed a first piece that made the next decision easier.
